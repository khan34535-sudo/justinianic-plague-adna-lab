    Poinar Lab Analytical Workflow

    Ancient DNA pipeline · from bone to genome

    // Lab Pipeline

    1
    Clean-Room Sampling
    Ancient DNA work occurs in dedicated clean rooms. Contamination control: full-body suits, UV irradiation, bleach, and negative controls at every step.
    2
    Tooth / Petrous Extraction
    Dental pulp chambers and petrous temporal bones are the densest sources of endogenous DNA. Teeth are cut at the cementoenamel junction; pulp is drilled out.
    3
    Bone Powder Lysis
    Powder is incubated with EDTA and proteinase K to release DNA. Ancient DNA is short, damaged, and present in trace amounts.
    4
    Library Preparation
    DNA fragments are end-repaired, adapter-ligated, and indexed. UDG treatment removes characteristic ancient cytosine deamination damage.
    5
    Targeted Capture Enrichment
    RNA baits "fish out" Y. pestis DNA from the background of host and environmental DNA. This is Poinar's signature technique for plague detection.
    6
    Sequencing & Phylogenetics
    High-throughput sequencing. Reads are mapped to reference genomes. Phylogenetic trees place the ancient strain in evolutionary context.

    // Why Ancient DNA

    Historical texts describe the Justinianic Plague — but they are limited by the biases of their authors, the vagaries of transmission, and the absence of direct biological evidence. Ancient DNA bypasses all of that: it reads the pathogen's own genome from the bodies of its victims.

    Poinar's work integrates:

    • Genomics — reconstructing the pathogen's full genome
    • Phylogenetics — placing it on the evolutionary tree
    • Radiocarbon dating — anchoring it in absolute time
    • Bioarchaeology — reading demography from the bones
    • Isotopes — tracking mobility and diet
